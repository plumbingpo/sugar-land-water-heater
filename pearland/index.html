Keeping Your Pearland Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

Common Water Heater Problems in Pearland

In Pearland, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units burn out heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
